# Emergency Medical Triage Protocols & First-Aid Guidelines (WHO & START Protocol Standards)

## PROTOCOL 1: ANAPHYLAXIS & SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION
- **Clinical Presentation**: Sudden onset (minutes to hours) of skin/mucosal involvement (hives, flushing, angioedema) PLUS respiratory compromise (dyspnea, wheezing, stridor, hypoxemia SpO2 < 92%) OR reduced blood pressure (systolic BP < 90 mmHg) or associated symptoms of end-organ dysfunction (syncope, incontinence).
- **Triage Urgency Tier**: RED (Resuscitation / Emergent - Immediate clinical intervention required).
- **Immediate Management**:
  1. Administer Epinephrine (Adrenaline) 1:1000 IM (0.3mg to 0.5mg in adults, 0.15mg in pediatric patients) into mid-outer thigh.
  2. Maintain airway; place patient in recumbent position with feet elevated unless respiratory distress dictates sitting position.
  3. Administer high-flow supplemental oxygen (10-15 L/min via non-rebreather mask).
  4. Establish IV access for crystalloid fluid bolus if hypotensive.
  5. Re-evaluate every 5 minutes. Prepare second epinephrine dose if no improvement in 5-15 minutes.

## PROTOCOL 2: ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME (ACS) & CHEST PAIN
- **Clinical Presentation**: Retrosternal chest pressure, heaviness, squeezing, or radiating pain to jaw, neck, left arm, or epigastrium. Associated symptoms: diaphoresis, nausea, dyspnea, lightheadedness.
- **Triage Urgency Tier**: RED (Emergent).
- **Immediate Management**:
  1. Rest in comfortable semi-Fowler position.
  2. Administer chewable Aspirin 160mg - 325mg immediately (unless allergic or acute hemorrhage suspected).
  3. Administer Nitroglycerin 0.4mg sublingual every 5 mins (up to 3 doses) IF systolic BP > 100 mmHg and no right ventricular infarction suspected.
  4. Continuous vital signs monitoring (BP, HR, SpO2, 12-lead ECG if available).
  5. Rapid triage transfer for reperfusion therapy.

---

## PROTOCOL 3: ACUTE RESPIRATORY DISTRESS & ASTHMA EXACERBATION
- **Clinical Presentation**: Tachypnea (Respiratory Rate > 30 bpm), inability to speak full sentences, intercostal retractions, cyanosis, SpO2 < 92% on room air, silent chest or severe wheezing.
- **Triage Urgency Tier**: RED (Severe respiratory distress) / YELLOW (Moderate asthma exacerbation, SpO2 92-95%, able to speak short phrases).
- **Immediate Management**:
  1. Administer inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonist (Salbutamol/Albuterol 2.5mg - 5mg via nebulizer or MDI with spacer).
  2. Supplemental oxygen targeting SpO2 94-98% (or 88-92% in chronic hypercapnic patients).
  3. Systemic Corticosteroids (Prednisolone 40-50mg orally or IV Hydrocortisone 100mg).
  4. Continuous SpO2 monitoring.

---

## PROTOCOL 4: HYPOVOLEMIC & SEPTIC SHOCK / HEMORRHAGE
- **Clinical Presentation**: Tachycardia (HR > 120 bpm), hypotension (systolic BP < 90 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg), altered mental status, cool clammy extremities, weak peripheral pulses, delayed capillary refill (> 3 seconds).
- **Triage Urgency Tier**: RED (Immediate / Resuscitation).
- **Immediate Management**:
  1. Hemorrhage Control: Apply direct pressure, pressure dressings, or arterial tourniquet for limb exsanguination.
  2. Fluid Resuscitation: Rapid IV/IO administration of warm Normal Saline or Ringer's Lactate (20 mL/kg bolus).
  3. Keep patient warm to prevent hypothermia-induced coagulopathy.
  4. Urgent surgical / trauma team notification.

---

## PROTOCOL 5: ACUTE FEVER & SUSPECTED INFECTION (SEPSIS PROTOCOL)
- **Clinical Presentation**: Fever (> 38.5°C) or Hypothermia (< 36.0°C), Heart Rate > 90 bpm, Respiratory Rate > 20 bpm, elevated or low WBC count. Red-flag sepsis signs: altered mental status, BP < 90 mmHg, lactate > 2 mmol/L.
- **Triage Urgency Tier**: YELLOW (Priority) if vitals stable; RED if septic shock signs present.
- **Immediate Management**:
  1. Measure lactate and obtain blood cultures before antimicrobial therapy if feasible.
  2. Administer empirical broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour.
  3. Rapid fluid resuscitation (30 mL/kg crystalloids) for hypotension or lactate >= 4 mmol/L.
  4. Paracetamol 1000mg PO/IV for symptomatic fever control.

---

## PROTOCOL 6: MINOR TRAUMA, CUTS & ROUTINE ILLNESS
- **Clinical Presentation**: Controlled minor bleeding, localized abrasions, isolated closed sprains/strains, low-grade fever without systemic compromise, stable vitals (SpO2 > 96%, HR 60-90, BP 110-130/70-85).
- **Triage Urgency Tier**: GREEN (Non-Urgent / Routine).
- **Immediate Management**:
  1. Wound cleansing with sterile saline, application of topical antiseptic and sterile dressing.
  2. Standard oral analgesia (Paracetamol 500-1000mg or Ibuprofen 400mg PO).
  3. Patient education on infection signs and routine follow-up.
